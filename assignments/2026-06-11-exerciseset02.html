For some of these exercises you will need to recall the the arc length formula for a parametrization \(\theta(t)\), \[L(t)= \int_{t_0}^t \|\theta'(u)\| du\]

  1. Consider a circle of radius \(r\). Choose a parametrization of speed \(\nu\) for it. Compute the magnitude of the acceleration of this parametrization.

  2. Using the arc length forumla introduced in the lecture, compute the arc length of a circle of radius \(r\), any given line, and an ellipse. In each case you will also have to find a suitable parametrization.

  3. Let \(\theta_1(t)\) denote a parametrization defining a curve. If I define a new parametrization \(\theta_2(t)= \theta_1(\tau(t))\), where \(\tau\) is some reparametrization, then this is also another parametrization on the same curve. Choose a point \(p\) on the curve.

    1. How does the velocity of the first parametrization at point \(p\) relate with the velocity of the second parametrization at the same point \(p\)?

    2. Show that if \(\tau(t)\) is the arc length function introduced in the lecture, then the velocity of \(\theta_2\) would always have magnitude 1. Use the integral definition. Do not just describe it intuitively. Use calculus.
