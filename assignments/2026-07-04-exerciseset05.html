  1. Given two surface patches \(\sigma_{1} : U_1 \to \mathbb{R}^3\) and \(\sigma_{2} : U_2 \to \mathbb{R}^3\), whose images cover the same part of the a given surface \(S\) in \(\mathbb{R}^3\), how would you relate them? In other words, define a “coordinate transformation” map to relate \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\) just as we defined a reparametrization to relate two parametrizations \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) of curves.

  2. Rememember \(\mathbf{n}(p)\), the unit normal to the surface at \(p\), which is defined as \(\frac{\sigma_x \times \sigma_y}{\|\sigma_{x}\times \sigma_y}\|\).

    1. Do you think it depends on the surface patch \(\sigma\)? After all, if you change \(\sigma\), the vectors \(\sigma_x\) and \(\sigma_y\) change.

    2. To find out the how \(\mathbf{n}(\theta(t))\) changes along the parametrization \(\theta\), i.e. to know \(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbf{n}(\theta(t))\), it is enough to figure out \(\mathbf{n}_x\) and \(\mathbf{n}_y\). Why is that enough?

    3. Show that \(\mathbf{n}_x\) and \(\mathbf{n}_y\) must be tangent to the surface. Therefore, they are of the form \(\mathbf{n}_x = a \sigma_x + b\sigma_{y}\) and \(\mathbf{n}_y = c \sigma_x + d\sigma_{y}\) for some scalars \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and \(d\).

    4. Find out the scalars \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and \(d\) in terms of the components of the fundamental forms, \(E\), \(F\), \(G\), \(L\), \(M\), and \(N\). You might find the computation easier if you arrange the coefficients in a matrix \[\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\] You will then be able to express it as a matrix multiplication of two matrices which have \(E\), \(F\), \(G\), \(L\), \(M\), \(N\) in their entries.

  3. Consider the sphere of radius 1. Show that any curve that lies on the sphere and has geodesic curvature 0, must be a great circle. To do this, you must first define a suitable surface patch on part of the sphere.
